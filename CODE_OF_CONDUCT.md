# Code of conduct

## The short version

Everyone working on this project should be able to ask a question, make a
mistake, or disagree with a decision without it costing them anything socially.

## What that looks like in practice

- Critique the code, not the person who wrote it. "This misses the empty case"
  rather than "you always forget this".
- Assume people are doing their best with the context they had. Most bad code
  is a reasonable decision made with less information than you have now.
- Say why. A change request without a reason is hard to act on and easy to
  resent.
- "I don't know" and "I was wrong" are good contributions. A project where
  nobody says either is a project where people are hiding things.
- Nobody owns a directory. Expect your work to be reviewed, and review others'
  the way you would want yours read.

## Not acceptable

Harassment, discrimination or personal attacks; sexualised language or
imagery; publishing anyone's private information; sustained disruption of
discussions; or deliberately undermining another person's work.

## Scope

This applies wherever the project happens — issues, pull requests, commit
messages, review comments, and the conversations around them.

## If something goes wrong

Raise it with a maintainer, or with the course staff if a maintainer is the
problem. Reports will be taken seriously and kept as private as the situation
allows. Maintainers are responsible for acting on them, and are held to this
document at least as strictly as everyone else.
